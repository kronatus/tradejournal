# No free API gives chains and Greeks cleanly

**The honest headline: nothing satisfies all three of your constraints at once.** A source that is $0 forever, requires no account with any broker, is contractually licensed for programmatic use, and returns both option chains *and* Greeks does not exist in September 2026. The constraint that breaks the field is **chains**, not Greeks and not price. Marketdata.app's "Free Forever" plan is the cleanest fit on paper — $0, no credit card, no brokerage, native OCC symbols, `delta/gamma/theta/vega` inline — but it bills **one credit per contract returned** against a **100-credit/day** budget, so browsing a chain is arithmetically impossible on free; it works only for repricing a known list of OCC symbols you already hold. The one provider that genuinely delivers chains *and* pre-computed Greeks at $0 is **Alpaca**, on a **15-minute-delayed indicative feed**, and the price is an **unfunded paper account opened with an email address, no KYC and no funding** — a materially weaker requirement than Tradier's, which states flatly that "if you are not a Tradier Brokerage account holder, we are unable to provide you with any real-time data solution" ([Tradier docs](https://docs.tradier.com/docs/getting-started)). Below that sits an unlicensed tier — Cboe's `cdn.cboe.com` JSON and Yahoo via `yahoo-finance2` — that is technically excellent and contractually prohibited. The decision is therefore not "which API" but "which of three compromises": a paper brokerage account, a scraped endpoint, or no chain browsing at all.

> **Methodology caveat — read before acting on any number here.** This session's egress proxy returned **HTTP 403 organization-policy denials on nearly every official provider domain**: `marketdata.app`, `alphavantage.co`, `polygon.io`, `massive.com`, `docs.alpaca.markets`, `cboe.com`, `cdn.cboe.com` and others. **Not one official pricing page or API reference was read directly.** Every quota, price and field name below comes from a search index's summary of those pages. Treat them as well-sourced hearsay, not verification. A verified/unverified table and the three cheap tests that would settle the rest appear near the end.

## Marketdata.app's per-contract credit meter makes chain browsing arithmetically impossible

Marketdata.app is the provider that should have won, and the reason it doesn't is worth stating precisely because it is counterintuitive. The plan is real and permanent: **"Free Forever," $0/month, no credit card, 100 API credits per day, 24-hour-delayed data**, with 15-minute delay explicitly excluded ([Free Forever Plan](https://www.marketdata.app/docs/account/plans/free-forever/)). The daily counter **resets at 9:30 AM Eastern**, the NYSE opening bell, not at midnight ([Rate Limits](https://www.marketdata.app/docs/api/rate-limiting/)) — which quietly means a 2 AM cron draws on the *previous* day's bucket. The option chain endpoint "provides pricing data, greeks, implied volatility, and contract details for every option trading in the US" ([Options Data](https://www.marketdata.app/data/options/)), identifiers are **required to be current-format OCC** (`AAPL230728C00200000`), and there are dedicated `expirations` and `strikes` endpoints alongside the full chain ([Expirations](https://www.marketdata.app/docs/api/options/expirations/)).

The widely-feared null-Greeks trap turns out **not** to apply to you: the docs state that on any request including a `date` parameter, `iv`, `delta`, `gamma`, `theta` and `vega` come back **null on every plan**, and that those fields "are only populated on current (real-time, delayed or end-of-day) chains" ([Option Chain](https://www.marketdata.app/docs/api/options/chain/)). An undated snapshot — exactly what a journal wants — returns real Greeks. But the undated request is precisely the one billed **per option symbol in the response**, and the docs' own worked example is brutal: requesting all strikes and expirations for SPX (22,718 contracts) **costs 22,718 credits** ([Rate Limits](https://www.marketdata.app/docs/api/rate-limiting/)). Dated requests are billed at a gentle 1 credit per 1,000 symbols — but those are the ones with null Greeks. The flat-rate escape hatch, `feed=cached` at 1 credit per request regardless of contract count ([Data Feed](https://www.marketdata.app/docs/api/universal-parameters/feed)), is **paid-plans-only**: free and trial accounts requesting cached mode receive **HTTP 402 Payment Required** and "will always receive delayed data" ([Data Mode](https://www.marketdata.app/docs/api/universal-parameters/mode/)).

So the meter closes the loop against you in both directions. Cheap chains have no Greeks; chains with Greeks cost a credit per strike; the flat-rate fix is gated. **100 credits/day buys roughly 100 individual contract quotes** — comfortably enough to reprice 20–50 open legs daily, and not enough for a single unfiltered SPY chain even once. If your requirement were only "quotes with Greeks for contracts I already hold," Marketdata.app would be the answer outright, at $0, with no account anywhere and no code changes beyond a base URL and a token. Because you also need expirations and strikes, it is a **partial pass demoted to a fallback**.

## Alpaca trades a funded brokerage account for an unfunded paper one

Alpaca is the only candidate that clears both halves of the data requirement on a genuinely free plan, and the question it forces is whether "no brokerage account" was the real constraint or a proxy for "no funding, no KYC, no money at risk." The Basic plan "serves as the default option for both Paper and Live trading accounts, ensuring all users can access essential data with zero cost," including "for options only the indicative feed" ([About Market Data API](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/about-market-data-api)). That indicative feed "is free for options and is a derivative of the OPRA feed with 15-minute delayed quotes" ([Historical Option Data](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/historical-option-data)). A dedicated **option chain endpoint** exists ([Option chain reference](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/reference/optionchain)), and the **snapshots endpoint "provides the latest trade, latest quote and greeks for each given contract symbol"** ([Snapshots reference](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/reference/optionsnapshots)). Critically, **"all greeks are pre-computed and always included — there is no optional parameter needed to retrieve them,"** though they live **only** on snapshot endpoints, never on bars/trades/quotes ([alpaca-py data models](https://alpaca.markets/sdks/python/api_reference/data/models.html)).

The account distinction is the whole argument. **A paper account "typically requir[es] just an email address and a few basic details," while a live account demands "identity verification (Know Your Customer – KYC) and linking a funding source"** ([How to Start Paper Trading](https://alpaca.markets/learn/start-paper-trading)); the paper account issues its own API key from the dashboard ([Connect to Alpaca API](https://alpaca.markets/learn/connect-to-alpaca-api)). Against Tradier's requirement of a *funded brokerage relationship*, that is a different category of obligation — no money moves, no SSN, no account application. The honest counterweights are two. First, it is still an account with a broker-dealer affiliate, so if your objection was structural rather than financial, this fails on principle and the decision is yours, not mine. Second, **the Greeks are computed on indicative data, not the authoritative OPRA feed, so they differ from what a paid OPRA subscriber sees** ([TradersPost](https://blog.traderspost.io/article/alpaca-paper-trading)) — for a journal reporting portfolio delta and theta bleed, a materially irrelevant difference; for anything claiming precision, a real one.

Set that against what the rest of the market does. **Massive (the renamed Polygon.io, effective 2025-10-30, with keys and accounts unchanged** — [Polygon.io is Now Massive](https://massive.com/blog/polygon-is-now-massive)) does return Greeks and IV inline on its option contract snapshot ([Option Contract Snapshot](https://massive.com/docs/rest/options/snapshots/option-contract-snapshot)), settling a contradiction from earlier research — you do not compute them yourself. But its free Basic tier is **5 API calls per minute** ([request limits](https://massive.com/knowledge-base/article/what-is-the-request-limit-for-massives-restful-apis)) and, decisively, **"the free Basic tier of every product is REST only: the WebSockets and the flat files start at the paid tiers, and so do trades, quotes and snapshots"** ([subscriptions FAQ](https://massive.com/knowledge-base/article/what-are-the-different-massive-subscriptions-i-can-use)) — which excludes the exact endpoint family the Greeks live on. Cheapest path to Greeks there is **$29/month**. The rest of the sweep failed for one dominant reason, free-but-equities-only: **IEX Cloud was retired outright on 2024-08-31** ([Tiingo](https://www.tiingo.com/blog/iex-cloud-alternatives/)); **Intrinio has no permanent free tier and starts around $150/month**; EODHD sells options with Greeks as a **separate marketplace add-on** ([EODHD Marketplace](https://eodhd.com/marketplace/unicornbay/options)); Databento offers **$125 in credits**, not a free tier; Tiingo and MarketStack have **no options product at all**; ORATS, Barchart and Nasdaq Data Link's ORATS surfaces are commercial; and **dxFeed's free access runs through a tastytrade brokerage account** — the precise coupling you are leaving. Finnhub, Twelve Data, FMP and ThetaData remain unresolved but presumptively fail, with no citation found showing an options-chain-with-Greeks endpoint on any free plan.

## The unlicensed tier hands you everything and promises nothing

Two sources solve your problem technically and fail it contractually, and they deserve a straight description rather than either a recommendation or a lecture.

**Cboe's delayed-quotes JSON is the single best technical fit found anywhere.** The endpoint behind the public quote table is `https://cdn.cboe.com/api/global/delayed_quotes/options/{SYMBOL}.json`, with an underscore prefix for index symbols (`_SPX`, `_XSP`); `data.options` carries the chain array and `data.current_price` the underlying spot. OpenBB's production Cboe provider renames `iv`→`implied_volatility` and `theo`→`theoretical_price` and **passes `delta`, `gamma`, `theta`, `vega` and `rho` through as-is without computing them** ([OpenBB cboe options_chains.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenBB-finance/OpenBB/develop/openbb_platform/providers/cboe/openbb_cboe/models/options_chains.py)). That is chains, expirations, strikes, Greeks, IV and spot in **one unauthenticated call with no key, no account and no quota**. And the pages it backs state that it is "strictly prohibited to download delayed quote table data from the website by using auto-extraction programs/queries and/or software," that Cboe "will block IP addresses of all parties who attempt to do so," and that downloading "in any other way than by manual ticker symbol entry is strictly prohibited" ([Cboe Option Delayed Quotes](https://www.cboe.com/delayed_quotes/option/)). Cboe sells IV and Greeks as an analytics product ([Hanweck](https://www.cboe.com/services/analytics/hanweck/implied_volatility/)) and on DataShop ([Option Quote Intervals](https://datashop.cboe.com/option-quote-intervals)), which is why the free feed is unsanctioned rather than merely undocumented. The practical enforcement mechanism is technical — an IP block — not judicial, and Vercel's shared serverless egress cuts both ways: you are unlikely to be individually identified at low volume, and you inherit every other tenant's reputation. This is a decision to make knowingly. It forecloses ever making the app public or commercial on this data, and it should not be written into the codebase as though it were a licensed API.

**Yahoo via `yahoo-finance2` is the weaker but better-behaved unlicensed option.** The library is at v4 with ~1,647 commits and active CI ([gadicc/yahoo-finance2](https://github.com/gadicc/yahoo-finance2)), and its options module returns `expirationDates` and `strikes` at the result level with `impliedVolatility`, `bid`, `ask`, `lastPrice`, `strike`, `openInterest`, `volume` and `contractSymbol` per contract — and **no `delta`, `gamma`, `theta` or `vega` fields at all**, despite the module blurb mentioning Greeks ([JSR options module docs](https://jsr.io/@gadicc/yahoo-finance2/doc/modules/options)). That gap is exactly the one your repo already closes: feed Yahoo's per-contract IV into the existing `bsmGreeks()` and skip the IV solve entirely. Yahoo's ToS likewise prohibit automated collection and building substitute databases ([Yahoo Terms of Service](https://legal.yahoo.com/us/en/frontier/terms/otos/index.html)). Its saving grace is the **failure mode**: the library validates payloads and throws `FailedYahooValidationError` on schema drift rather than returning silently wrong numbers, and recent breakages closed fast — **issue #1024 opened Aug 7, 2026 and #1025 on Aug 9, 2026, both closed and released** ([issue list](https://github.com/gadicc/yahoo-finance2/issues?q=is%3Aissue+sort%3Aupdated-desc)). The known operational hazard is the cookie/crumb bootstrap returning **429 Too Many Requests** ([issue #977](https://github.com/gadicc/yahoo-finance2/issues/977)); the documented anti-pattern is clearing the session on a 429 and immediately re-fetching a crumb, which turns one failure into thousands of hits. At tens of requests per day this should never fire.

### Alpha Vantage is the one option that can silently poison your database

Alpha Vantage deserves separate billing because its failure mode is worse than a failure. The free key is genuine — **25 API requests per day, lifetime access, email signup, no brokerage, no credit card** ([Alpha Vantage](https://www.alphavantage.co/)) — and `HISTORICAL_OPTIONS` genuinely documents full chains with **IV and delta, gamma, theta, vega and rho**, sorted by expiration then strike, back 15+ years ([API Documentation](https://www.alphavantage.co/documentation/)). But **`REALTIME_OPTIONS` requires the 600 or 1,200 requests/minute premium plan, and "the standard 75 requests/minute plan and free accounts will receive placeholder/demo data instead of real market data"** ([Premium API Key](https://www.alphavantage.co/premium/)). Returning fabricated data rather than a 402 or 403 is catastrophic for this specific app: your quotes route would parse it, your Zod schema would accept it because the shape is valid, and `app/api/quotes/route.ts` would write **invented Greeks into `current_net_delta/gamma/theta/vega` in Postgres**, overwriting real history with numbers that look plausible forever after. Whether `HISTORICAL_OPTIONS` on a free key returns real data or the same placeholders is the one thing the research could not pin down — and it is the single most worthwhile empirical test on this list, because the answer flips Alpha Vantage between "usable EOD backfill" and "actively dangerous." Independent testing also reports its **IV is roughly 2x off for contracts with DTE ≤ 3**, while delta looks correct ([Oyamori](https://oyamori.com/learning/options-data-api-alphavantage-alpaca/)). The entry price for real options entitlement is **$49.99/month for EOD options**, with real-time only at $199.99–$249.99.

## The build is small, and the two missing pieces are both in pricing.ts

Assume the Alpaca path. The minimal architecture is one route handler and two small additions to code you already have tested.

| Need | Primary (Alpaca) | Fallback (unlicensed / local) |
|---|---|---|
| Expirations + strikes | Alpaca option chain endpoint | Cboe `data.options`, or Yahoo `expirationDates`/`strikes` |
| Per-contract quote | Alpaca snapshot (`latest trade`, `latest quote`) | Cboe chain rows, or Yahoo `bid`/`ask` |
| Greeks | Alpaca snapshot, **pre-computed, always present** | Cboe `delta/gamma/theta/vega/rho`, or `bsmGreeks()` fed by Yahoo IV |
| Implied volatility | Alpaca snapshot IV | Yahoo `impliedVolatility`; else solve from bid/ask **mid** |
| Underlying spot | Alpaca equity quote (IEX on Basic) | Cboe `data.current_price` |
| Risk-free rate | FRED `DGS1MO`/`DGS3MO`, **free self-service API key** ([FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/docs/api/api_key.html), [DGS3MO](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS3MO)) | hardcoded constant |

Per CLAUDE.md the fetch belongs in `app/api/*` and the response needs a Zod schema in `lib/schemas.ts` (CLAUDE.md still writes these as `src/...`, but this repo has no `src/` directory), and the existing gotcha applies — substitute a fresh ISO timestamp rather than trusting a vendor's `updated_at` against strict `.datetime()`. One convention trap specific to this data: **Greeks are dimensionless ratios and must not be cents-converted**, even though the codebase's rule is cents-everywhere for money.

Two code gaps are real and were confirmed by reading the repo, not inferred. **`lib/pricing.ts` has no implied-volatility solver** — grep for `impliedVol|newton|bisect` across `lib/` returns nothing — so any path that has a price but no IV (a Cboe outage, an illiquid Yahoo strike with a junk IV, a future source that ships price only) currently has no way to produce Greeks. The solver shape is Newton-Raphson on `bsmPrice(σ) − marketPrice` using `vega` as the derivative, both of which already exist, seeded near 0.3 or via Brenner-Subrahmanyam. **The warning that matters: vega → 0 both deep ITM/OTM and near expiry, which makes bare Newton diverge or return nonsense.** A bounded bisection fallback on σ ∈ [0.001, 5.0] with an iteration cap and an explicit "unsolvable" return rather than `NaN` is mandatory, not optional — it is the most common bug in home-rolled IV solvers. Always solve from the **bid/ask midpoint**, never `lastPrice`, and discard contracts whose spread is a large fraction of the mid; quote quality, not model choice, dominates the error. Second, **`BsmParams` is `{S, K, r, T, sigma, type}` with no dividend-yield `q`**, so the implementation is pure Black-Scholes with no carry adjustment. Ignoring a ~1.2% ETF yield on a 30-day option is a small but *systematic* bias, and the Merton continuous-dividend extension — multiply `S` by `e^(−qT)` in `d1`/`d2` and in the price and delta terms — is roughly a ten-line change. Both additions fall under the rule that calculations in `lib/` must have Vitest tests. American early exercise is **not** worth implementing: for the short-dated near-the-money contracts a journal holds, the BSM-vs-American delta gap is far smaller than bid/ask-induced IV noise.

The sharpest integration risk is the one your own docs already name. **Cboe and Yahoo both emit standard OCC symbols with the strike in the dollars×1000 convention** — `SPY260529C00755000` — which is the Tradier-style format, **not** this project's storage format `O:SPY260529C00075500` with the strike in cents. `OCC_SYMBOLOGY.md` opens by calling that encoding "the #1 Source of Bugs," `toTradierSymbol()` in `lib/quotes.ts` is the canonical converter, `components/strategy-form.tsx` has already diverged from it with prefixed-plus-cents semantics, and **`lib/quotes.ts` and `lib/snapshots.ts` have zero test coverage** while `nextsteps.md` flags the conversion as "the highest-risk integration boundary." Whatever provider you pick, the first commit should be tests on that converter, not the new fetch. Alpaca's and Marketdata.app's OCC compatibility means the converter is reused rather than re-derived; Massive's `O:`-prefixed variant would be a trivial strip, if it were ever in play.

Three tests, each free and under ten minutes, collapse most of that uncertainty. **First, open an Alpaca paper account and call the option chain and snapshot endpoints for SPY** — this confirms in one shot whether chains and pre-computed Greeks really arrive on Basic, what the symbol format is, and how stale the indicative feed actually is. **Second, fire one `HISTORICAL_OPTIONS` call with a free Alpha Vantage key and inspect the payload for demo markers** (implausibly round numbers, a fixed date, an "Information" or "Note" envelope) — this is the difference between a usable backfill source and one that would silently fabricate Greeks in your database. **Third, `curl` the Cboe JSON once for SPY and once for a mid-cap single name**, which settles the field names, whether coverage extends beyond Cboe-listed and index products, and whether your egress IP is already blocked — before any code is written against it.

## Conclusion

The useful reframe is that "free options data" has quietly split into two products with different economics, and the split runs straight through your requirements. **Per-contract quotes with Greeks are nearly a commodity** — Marketdata.app gives you a hundred a day for nothing, Alpaca gives you them on an email-only account, even Robinhood's MCP returns them verbatim. **Chains are the expensive product**, because a chain is where the vendor's full surface lives, and every free tier meters exactly that: Marketdata.app per contract, Massive behind the snapshot paywall, Alpha Vantage behind entitlement, Cboe behind a prohibition. Notice that this maps cleanly onto how your journal actually behaves. Repricing open legs needs quotes for symbols you already know; browsing expirations and strikes is a *trade-entry* convenience, not a journaling requirement. If you can live with typing a strike instead of picking one from a dropdown, **Marketdata.app alone satisfies every hard constraint you stated, with no account anywhere, and the whole dilemma evaporates.**

If you do want chains, the recommendation is **Alpaca as primary, with Yahoo via `yahoo-finance2` feeding the existing `bsmGreeks()` as the named fallback, and Marketdata.app as the no-broker-at-all retreat** — and the deciding question is one only you can answer: whether your objection to Tradier was the funding and KYC, in which case Alpaca is a real escape, or the brokerage relationship as such, in which case it isn't and you are choosing between an unlicensed endpoint and no chains. Whichever way that goes, the highest-return work is not the vendor swap. It is what the architecture analysis already found: **no tests on the OCC conversion, a dead `current_value_cents` column, per-leg prices fetched and discarded, and no unrealized P/L anywhere in `lib/calculations.ts`.** Build the new adapter behind a source-tagged interface, persist which source produced each Greek alongside a fetch timestamp, refuse to render marks older than a threshold rather than showing a stale number, and keep the journal fully functional when every feed is down. A journal whose core value depends on a free quote feed is one deprecation notice away from being a spreadsheet.
